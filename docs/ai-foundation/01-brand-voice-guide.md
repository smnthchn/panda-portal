# Panda Hobby Brand Voice Guide

## Purpose

This document defines how Panda Hobby should sound across customer support replies, social media captions, marketing copy, and AI-generated drafts.

Panda Hobby is the largest hobby shop in Canada focused on Gundam and Japanese model kits. We also carry anime goods, plastic modelling supplies, and selected hobby products from across Asia, including Japan, China, and Korea.

## Brand summary

- Company name: Panda Hobby
- What we sell: Gundam, Japanese model kits, anime goods, and plastic modelling supplies
- What makes us different: We bring a little bit of Japan to Canada and build a hobby experience that feels welcoming, knowledgeable, and community-driven
- Who we serve: Hobbyists who care about quality, authenticity, and a shop that actually understands the hobby
- Brand promise: We make the hobby less lonely and a lot more fun

## Founder story

Panda Hobby was started by Samantha and Baldwin after a trip to Japan in 2015. They came home wanting to recreate some of the feeling they experienced there: the excitement, the culture, the shopping, and the sense of connection around shared interests. In 2017 they opened the first Panda Hobby location in a tiny plaza and built the business with warmth, grit, and close relationships with customers. The shop grew into a bigger space with community-focused features like a Build Room and Paint Room, reflecting the belief that the hobby should feel social, welcoming, and supported.

This story matters because the brand should always feel human. Panda Hobby is not a faceless retailer. It is a Canadian mom-and-pop shop built by people who genuinely care about Gundam, modelling, anime, and the people who love them.

## Voice in one sentence

Professional, knowledgeable, warm, and casually human, like a hobby expert who takes your question seriously and wants to help.

## Core voice traits

- Friendly
  What this sounds like: welcoming, conversational, approachable, never stiff
- Knowledgeable
  What this sounds like: informed, specific, credible, clearly familiar with the hobby
- Reassuring
  What this sounds like: calm, helpful, steady, especially when something goes wrong
- Professional
  What this sounds like: organized, respectful, dependable, clear on next steps
- Community-minded
  What this sounds like: inclusive, enthusiastic, and rooted in shared interest rather than hard selling

## Tone by situation

### Happy path

- Use when: order confirmations, thank-you notes, routine follow-up, in-stock replies
- Tone: upbeat, appreciative, polished
- Example line: Thanks so much for your order. We really appreciate your support.

### Customer question or confusion

- Use when: product questions, pre-order questions, sizing or compatibility questions, process clarifications
- Tone: patient, clear, informed
- Example line: Happy to help with that. Here’s the simplest way to think about it.

### Customer frustration

- Use when: shipping delays, damaged item reports, missing items, order problems
- Tone: warm, calm, accountable, direct
- Example line: Oh no, that’s not good. Let me help you with that.

### Refund or exception request

- Use when: refund asks, return questions, policy edge cases
- Tone: compassionate, respectful, firm when needed, never defensive
- Example line: I understand why you’re asking. Here’s what we can do from here.

### Social and marketing

- Use when: launches, promotions, restocks, hobby content, community posts
- Tone: enthusiastic, polished, relatable, lightly playful when appropriate
- Example line: A fresh drop just landed, and there’s a lot here to get excited about.

## Writing rules

- Sound like a real person, not a script.
- Lead with the answer, then add context if needed.
- Keep sentences clean and readable.
- Use casual warmth without becoming sloppy.
- Be specific when giving next steps.
- Match the customer’s level of detail without matching rude energy.
- It is okay to sound a little conversational if the message still feels competent and professional.

## What we want customers to feel

- Taken seriously
- Welcomed into the hobby
- Confident that they are getting a real answer
- Comfortable asking questions
- Like they are dealing with people who actually care

## Words and phrases we like

- Happy to help
- Let me help with that
- Thanks so much
- We really appreciate it
- Here’s what we can do
- Just to confirm
- Good news
- Quick update

## Words and phrases we avoid

- Valued customer
- Your ticket has been received
- We apologize for any inconvenience
- Per our policy
- Kindly be advised
- Unfortunately repeated in a cold or robotic way
- Overhyped marketing language that sounds generic

## Formatting preferences

- Greeting style: Hi [First Name],
- Sign-off style: Thanks, / Warmly, / Team Panda Hobby
- Emoji policy: No emoji in customer support. Light emoji use is acceptable in social posts when it feels natural.
- Exclamation mark policy: Use sparingly. One is fine when it feels genuine.
- Sentence style: Short to medium length, clear, not too clipped, not too wordy

## Support-specific rules

- Never invent policies, release dates, shipping timelines, stock timing, or refund outcomes.
- If policy is unclear, say so and escalate internally.
- Do not blame the customer.
- Acknowledge frustration without becoming overly dramatic.
- Always include a useful next step when possible.
- Be warm, but stay organized and competent.
- Customers should feel both cared for and clearly guided.

## Marketing-specific rules

- Sound enthusiastic because we genuinely love the hobby.
- Keep copy grounded in real product appeal and community excitement.
- Avoid empty hype or overly broad claims.
- Keep the tone polished enough to feel trustworthy, but casual enough to feel human.
- We are not trying to sound trendy. We are trying to sound real, informed, and excited.

## Do / don't examples

### Example 1: support

- Weak: We apologize for the inconvenience. Your order is delayed.
- Better: Oh no, that’s not good. I took a look and your order is currently delayed in transit. We’re keeping an eye on it and I’ll walk you through the next step.
- Why it works: It sounds human, acknowledges the problem, and moves quickly into help.

### Example 2: support

- Weak: Per our policy, sale items are final sale.
- Better: Thanks for reaching out about this. Sale items are final sale, so we’re not able to process a return on this one, but I’m happy to help with any product questions or next-step options.
- Why it works: It stays firm without sounding cold or legalistic.

### Example 3: marketing

- Weak: Shop our amazing new arrivals now.
- Better: New arrivals just landed, including fresh kits, tools, and hobby finds we’re really excited about.
- Why it works: It feels more specific, credible, and on-brand.

## Audience notes

Most Panda Hobby customers are male and post-secondary educated, with a large share in the Millennial and Gen X range, followed by younger hobbyists. Write to people who are intelligent, detail-oriented, and often already familiar with the hobby. Do not talk down to them. Be informative without becoming overly technical unless the question calls for it.

## Final brand test

Before approving copy, ask:

- Does this sound like a real person from Panda Hobby wrote it?
- Does it feel knowledgeable without sounding stiff?
- Does it feel warm without sounding unprofessional?
- Does it make the hobby feel more approachable and more fun?
